# Pay periods

A pay period is the stored dates of one pay cycle of one scheme in one month: the cycle dates, the four attendance windows
(overtime, no-pay leave, late, attendance allowance), the range the pay date may fall in, and whether EPF / MSPS / ETF are deducted in that cycle.
Pay inputs and pay runs pick a stored period (scheme, then period) instead of free dates.

## Install
1. Copy over the project. Overwrites: app/Providers/AppServiceProvider.php (it already contains the loans and hire purchase route groups),
   config/permissions.php and config/navigation.php (both already contain the hire purchases module).
   New: 1 migration, app/Models/PayPeriod.php, app/Payroll/PayPeriodPlan.php, app/Services/PayPeriodService.php,
   app/Http/Controllers/PayPeriodController.php, routes/pay_periods.php, resources/views/payroll/periods/index.blade.php, tests/Unit/Payroll/PayPeriodPlanTest.php.
   If you have NOT installed the hire purchases package, do that first (these three overwritten files include it).
2. `php artisan migrate`
3. `php artisan db:seed --class=AccessControlSeeder`   (adds the pay-periods.* permissions to Super Admin)
4. `php artisan optimize:clear` and `php artisan route:clear`
5. Give roles the permissions. Payroll > Pay periods > choose the scheme and tax year > Generate periods.
6. Tests: `php artisan test tests/Unit/Payroll/PayPeriodPlanTest.php`

## Permissions
pay-periods.view (see periods of your schemes), pay-periods.generate (generate and refresh), pay-periods.close, pay-periods.reopen (separate).
Users see and manage only the periods of schemes allocated to them (schemes.all sees every scheme); out-of-scope pages are 404.

## Rules
* **Generated by hand, once a year per scheme.** Pick the scheme and the tax year (April to March, for example Y/A 2026/27) and press Generate periods.
  It creates every month and every cycle of that tax year. Each row shows its calendar month. The start month comes from config/statutory.php (tax_year_start_month).
* **Dates are copied from the scheme's pay settings in force in each month** (the version effective on the 1st of that month) and stored with the id of that
  version. A later change to the scheme settings never alters a stored period.
* A month with no pay settings in force is skipped and named in the message: add (or backdate) the scheme pay settings and generate again.
* **Safe to repeat**: periods that exist are left alone. If the scheme settings change mid-year, use **Refresh open periods**: open periods whose dates no
  longer match are updated, and open periods of a cycle the settings no longer have (for example you went from two cycles to one) are removed
  (a period that later tables use cannot be removed and is kept). Closed and locked periods are never changed; the message says how many differ.
* **Status**: Open, Closed, Locked. Close and reopen are separate permissions. Reopening needs a reason. Locked is set by the pay run
  (PayPeriodService::lock(), only from Closed) and cannot be reopened.
* **For pay inputs and the pay run**: call PayPeriodService::assertOpen($period) before changing anything that belongs to a period.
  Tables that reference pay_periods must use a restricting foreign key.
* Every generate, refresh, close, reopen and lock is audited.

## Decisions built in
Generated manually once a year per scheme; tax-year based; separate permissions for close and reopen; inputs only while a period is Open;
the year is a tax year (April to March).
